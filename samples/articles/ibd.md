# Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD): Symptoms, Diagnosis & Care

**Overview (first 100 words with keyword):**  
Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) — including Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis — causes chronic inflammation of the digestive tract. Typical **IBD symptoms** include persistent diarrhea, abdominal pain, blood in stool, and weight loss. Early diagnosis improves outcomes; management includes medication, lifestyle, and sometimes surgery.

## Common symptoms
- Persistent diarrhea often with blood
- Abdominal cramps and pain
- Weight loss and fatigue
- Extra-intestinal signs: joint pain, skin rashes

## How is IBD diagnosed?
Diagnosis involves:
- Blood tests (CRP, full blood count)
- Stool tests to rule out infection
- Colonoscopy with biopsy for definitive diagnosis
- Imaging (CT/MR enterography) for small bowel disease

## Treatment overview
- Aminosalicylates for mild UC
- Corticosteroids for flares (short term)
- Immunomodulators / biologics for moderate-severe disease
- Nutritional support and surgery for complications

## Diet & nutrition
- Avoid obvious triggers during flares (high-fiber may worsen symptoms)
- Use supplements if malabsorbed (iron, B12)
- Work with a dietitian for personalized plan

## When to see a doctor
Seek urgent care for:
- Heavy rectal bleeding
- Severe abdominal pain or fever
- Signs of bowel obstruction

## FAQs (5)
**Q1: Is IBD the same as IBS?**  
A: No. IBD involves intestinal inflammation visible on tests; IBS does not.

**Q2: Can IBD be cured?**  
A: Some patients require surgery; medical therapy can induce remission but long-term management is common.

**Q3: Are biologics safe?**  
A: Biologics reduce inflammation and are effective; discuss risks with a specialist.

**Q4: What tests confirm IBD?**  
A: Colonoscopy with biopsy is the gold standard.

**Q5: How do I manage diet during flares?**  
A: Low-residue diet and small frequent meals often help; consult a dietitian.
