# Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) — Symptoms, Treatment & Diet

**Overview (first 100 words with keyword):**  
Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) is a common gastrointestinal disorder that affects bowel habits and causes symptoms like abdominal pain, bloating, and changes in stool pattern. Early recognition of **IBS symptoms** helps with effective management and improves quality of life. This guide explains symptoms, causes, diet guidance, treatment options, and when to see a doctor.

## What are the common IBS symptoms?
- Recurrent abdominal pain related to bowel movements
- Bloating and gas
- Diarrhea, constipation, or alternating patterns
- Urgency or incomplete evacuation

## Causes and risk factors
IBS does not have one clear cause. Contributing factors include:
- Gut-brain axis changes
- Sensitivity to certain foods (FODMAPs)
- Previous gastrointestinal infections
- Stress and anxiety

## How is IBS diagnosed?
Diagnosis is clinical — your doctor will:
- Review symptom pattern (Rome IV criteria)
- Exclude alarm signs (weight loss, bleeding)
- Order blood tests, stool tests, or limited imaging if needed

## Diet & lifestyle guidance
- Try a low-FODMAP elimination for 4–8 weeks with a dietitian.
- Increase soluble fiber (psyllium) for constipation-predominant IBS.
- Limit caffeine, alcohol, and large fatty meals.
- Regular exercise and stress-management techniques help.

## Medical treatments (overview)
- Antispasmodics for pain
- Laxatives or anti-diarrheal agents as needed
- Low-dose antidepressants for visceral pain
- Probiotics — specific strains may help some patients

## When to see a doctor
Seek urgent care if:
- Severe abdominal pain
- Ongoing bleeding or black stools
- Unintended weight loss
For routine assessment, see your GP if symptoms persist or affect life quality.

## Comparison table — IBS vs other gut conditions

| Feature | IBS | IBD (Crohn’s / UC) |
|---|---:|---|
| Bleeding | Rare | Common |
| Weight loss | Uncommon | Common |
| Inflammation on tests | No | Yes |
| Urgency | Common | Common |

## FAQs (5)
**Q1: Can IBS be cured?**  
A: IBS is usually a long-term condition managed with diet, lifestyle, and medications; complete cure is uncommon. (Source: NIH)

**Q2: Is IBS dangerous?**  
A: IBS itself is not life-threatening but symptoms can greatly affect quality of life.

**Q3: What foods trigger IBS?**  
A: Common triggers include high-FODMAP foods, large fatty meals, and caffeine.

**Q4: Are probiotics helpful?**  
A: Some strains help; consult a clinician for tailored advice.

**Q5: When should I see a doctor?**  
A: See a doctor for alarm symptoms like bleeding, weight loss, or severe pain.
