# SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth): Signs, Tests & Treatment

**Overview (first 100 words with keyword):**  
Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO) occurs when excessive bacteria grow in the small intestine and cause **SIBO symptoms** such as bloating, gas, diarrhea, and nutrient malabsorption. Proper testing and targeted treatment improve outcomes.

## Common symptoms
- Persistent bloating and distension
- Excessive gas and belching
- Diarrhea or steatorrhea (fatty stools)
- Nutritional deficiencies (B12)

## Diagnosis
- Hydrogen/methane breath test is commonly used
- Consider small bowel aspirate in specialized centers
- Rule out underlying causes: motility disorders, structural issues

## Treatment options
- Antibiotic courses (rifaximin or others based on breath test)
- Dietary approaches: elemental diet short-term or low-FODMAP modifications
- Address underlying motility problems

## When to seek medical help
- Ongoing weight loss or severe digestive symptoms
- Signs of malabsorption or severe fatigue

## FAQs (5)
**Q1: What causes SIBO?**  
A: Causes include slow gut motility, anatomical changes after surgery, or repeated antibiotic use.

**Q2: How is SIBO tested?**  
A: Breath testing measuring hydrogen and methane after a sugar challenge is common.

**Q3: Is SIBO permanent?**  
A: With treatment and addressing underlying causes, many patients improve, though recurrence is possible.

**Q4: Should I change my diet?**  
A: Short-term dietary strategies can reduce symptoms; coordinate with a clinician.

**Q5: Are probiotics helpful?**  
A: Evidence is mixed — some patients benefit, others do not.
